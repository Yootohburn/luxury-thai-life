Where you are coming from

Advice shaped around your passport.

The right route, the way money moves and the tax questions all depend on nationality. These are the first things we look at.

Read the nationality notes

United States

  • US citizens are taxed on worldwide income wherever they live; Thai accounts bring FBAR and FATCA reporting.
  • Eligible for the 10-year O-X retirement visa at 50+.
  • LTR suits entrepreneurs and remote executives.
  • Owning a Thai company can add US filings: plan with a US tax adviser.

China & Hong Kong

  • Funds must move through licensed, traceable bank channels; mainland personal FX allowances apply.
  • We never assist with circumventing capital controls.
  • China does not recognise dual nationality.
  • LTR and Thailand Privilege are the usual starting points.

Middle East

  • Large families: LTR covers up to four dependants per applicant; others need their own visas.
  • Halal dining, mosques and private healthcare in Bangkok and Phuket.
  • International schools and villa privacy drive location choice.
  • Concierge, security and aviation support through vetted partners.

Europe & UK

  • Tax residency tests at home and in Thailand both apply; treaties decide ties.
  • Home-country inheritance and succession rules still reach Thai assets.
  • UK, DE, FR, CH, NL and the Nordics are O-X eligible at 50+.
  • Retirement visas require continuous health insurance.

Long-Term Resident

The LTR, and how property can count.

The only long-stay route where buying a home can help you qualify.

The Wealthy Global Citizen category asks for USD 1 million in assets and at least USD 500,000 invested in Thailand, through Thai government bonds, direct investment or Thai property. Wealthy Pensioners earning USD 40,000 to 80,000 a year can make up the difference with USD 250,000 invested the same way.

What counts, according to current guidance
  • Freehold condominium units held in your own name
  • Buildings or villas you own
  • Leasehold property with at least 10 years of the lease remaining
  • A combination of property, bonds and direct investment

Per BOI Announcement Por. 3/2568 (4 Feb 2025), as reported by Tilleke & Gibbins, 30 Apr 2026. MEDIUM

Conflicting sources

Some secondary guides still say only freehold condominiums qualify. The 2025 amendment reported by Tilleke & Gibbins includes buildings, villas and leaseholds with 10+ years remaining. We treat the BOI's written confirmation as the deciding source for each client.

Confirm with a licensed lawyer before you buy

  • Off-plan units: likely to count only once completed and transferred into your name
  • Mortgaged property: whether only your equity counts
  • Several properties combined to reach the threshold
  • How recently the purchase must have been made before you apply
  • Whether LTR years count towards the 3-year history for permanent residence

The BOI states that every condition, including the investment, must be kept for the life of the visa. Selling the property without replacing the investment can affect renewal. Fee: THB 50,000 for the 10-year visa when issued in Thailand. Reporting moves from every 90 days to once a year.

Thailand Privilege

Membership, not investment.

The official long-stay membership programme. It needs no income or asset test beyond the fee and a background check, and it includes airport fast-track and personal liaisons for reporting and banking. It has no link to property ownership.

Retirement

Retirement visas, O-A and O-X.

For clients aged 50 and over. The O-A is a one-year visa renewed annually, with deposit or income tests and health insurance. The O-X gives up to 10 years to citizens of 14 countries: the USA, UK, Canada, Australia, Japan, Germany, France, Italy, Switzerland, the Netherlands, Sweden, Norway, Denmark and Finland.

O-X fundsTHB 3M in a Thai fixed deposit, or THB 1.8M plus THB 1.2M annual income; at least THB 1.5M kept after year one
InsuranceRequired for the whole stay
WorkNot permitted, other than approved voluntary work

Retirement visas do not lead to permanent residence: there is no retirement category for PR.

Business & work

If you will work or run a company here.

Employees and executives usually hold a Non-B visa with a work permit, renewed with the job. BOI-promoted companies, the LTR Highly-Skilled Professional category and the SMART Visa offer faster routes in targeted industries; confirm which scheme is open to your sector.

Years of employment with Thai tax filings are the most common foundation for a permanent residence application.

Family

Moving the whole family.

Most routes let a spouse and children under 20 follow the main applicant. The LTR allows up to four dependants; parents and larger households may need their own visas. Since the Marriage Equality Act took effect in 2025, same-sex spouses are recognised as spouses.

Spouses of Thai nationals have their own visa route and a dedicated family category for permanent residence.

Permanent Residence

Can a property purchase lead to PR?

No, not directly.

There is no property category for Thai permanent residence. A home can support the LTR, and the life you build on that visa can later support an application, but the purchase itself earns nothing towards PR.

Citizenship

Thai citizenship cannot be bought.

Thailand has no citizenship-by-investment programme, no "golden passport" and no citizenship through property, whatever its value.

Naturalisation is possible under the Nationality Act for people who have lived here for many years. The usual sequence is permanent residence first, then at least five years of domicile, a regular occupation with Thai tax history, Thai language ability and good conduct. Spouses of Thai nationals have a shorter route. The Ministry of Interior decides, and approval is never guaranteed.

Check your home country's rules too: some countries, including China, do not allow dual nationality, and Thailand may ask applicants to declare they will renounce a previous nationality.

MEDIUM Confirm current requirements with a licensed Thai immigration lawyer.

What foreigners can own in Thailand

A clear account of condominiums, villas, land and company structures, including the ones we will not arrange. Reviewed 24 September 2026.

Condominiums

Freehold, in your own name.

Foreigners can own condominium units outright. Foreign ownership in each building is capped at 49% of the total unit floor area, so availability for foreign buyers varies by project.

FundsBrought into Thailand in foreign currency, with bank evidence of the transfer for the Land Office
TitleRegistered in your name at the Land Office
Transfer costsTransfer fee, stamp duty or specific business tax and withholding tax; who pays is negotiated
Inheritance & resaleUnits can be sold or inherited; a foreign heir must fit the foreign quota and rules. Confirm with a lawyer.
Proposals, not law

Plans to raise the foreign condominium quota to 75% and to allow 99-year leases were discussed in 2025. As of 24 September 2026 neither has been enacted.

Villas & houses

Own the house, lease the land.

A foreigner can own a building separately from the land beneath it. The land is usually held on a registered lease of up to 30 years.

Registered leaseUp to 30 years, registered at the Land Office
Renewal optionsContractual promises only. A 2025 Supreme Court ruling rejected stacked "30 + 30 + 30" structures.
SuperficiesA registered right to own buildings on another's land
UsufructA right to use land and its income for a period or for life

Leasehold houses with at least 10 years remaining may also count towards the LTR investment test. See LTR and property.

Land

Generally, no.

Foreign individuals cannot generally own land in Thailand. Section 96 bis of the Land Code allows up to one rai for residential use for a foreigner who invests at least THB 40 million in approved forms and keeps it there for at least five years, with the Minister of Interior's approval. It is rarely used, and we treat it as a specialist legal matter. MEDIUM

Companies

Thai companies and nominee risks.

The Foreign Business Act restricts foreign-majority companies, and a Thai-majority company is treated as foreign if its Thai shareholders are nominees. Holding land through nominee shareholders is illegal and carries criminal and civil exposure, including forced sale.

Structures we will not arrange or market
  • Nominee Thai shareholders used to hold land for a foreign buyer
  • "Guaranteed" lease renewals presented as ownership
  • Any structure described as a way around the land rules

Every purchase we support goes through an independent, licensed Thai law firm for title search, encumbrances, zoning and developer licences.

Relocate to Thailand

Schools, healthcare, money, tax and the rest of the move, coordinated with licensed partners.

Schools

Choosing the school first.

Bangkok has the deepest choice of British, American and IB schools, mostly along the Sukhumvit and Bang Na corridors; Phuket, Hua Hin and Chiang Mai have smaller international options. For families, we shortlist neighbourhoods around the school run and coordinate admissions timelines.

Healthcare

Private hospitals and insurance.

Internationally accredited private hospitals in Bangkok and Phuket offer English-speaking specialists and international patient centres. Several visas require continuous health insurance; we introduce licensed insurance brokers.

Banking & moving money

Moving large sums, properly.

Condominium purchases need foreign funds brought in through Thai banks with the right documentation. Every client provides source-of-funds and source-of-wealth evidence; developers, lawyers and banks run their own checks too.

Clients from mainland China

Personal foreign-exchange allowances and reporting rules apply to outbound transfers. Funds must move through licensed, traceable bank channels. We do not assist with circumventing capital controls.

Notes for our core markets

General orientation only. Home-country legal and tax advice is always needed.

United States

  • US citizens are taxed on worldwide income wherever they live; Thai accounts bring FBAR and FATCA reporting.
  • Eligible for the 10-year O-X retirement visa at 50+.
  • LTR suits entrepreneurs and remote executives.
  • Owning a Thai company can add US filings: plan with a US tax adviser.

China & Hong Kong

  • Funds must move through licensed, traceable bank channels; mainland personal FX allowances apply.
  • We never assist with circumventing capital controls.
  • China does not recognise dual nationality.
  • LTR and Thailand Privilege are the usual starting points.

Middle East

  • Large families: LTR covers up to four dependants per applicant; others need their own visas.
  • Halal dining, mosques and private healthcare in Bangkok and Phuket.
  • International schools and villa privacy drive location choice.
  • Concierge, security and aviation support through vetted partners.

Europe & UK

  • Tax residency tests at home and in Thailand both apply; treaties decide ties.
  • Home-country inheritance and succession rules still reach Thai assets.
  • UK, DE, FR, CH, NL and the Nordics are O-X eligible at 50+.
  • Retirement visas require continuous health insurance.
